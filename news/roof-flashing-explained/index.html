ROOF REPAIR GUIDE / PERTH

ROOF FLASHING EXPLAINED.

Prepared by Ellis Services Group · Updated 2026-10-10

Roof flashing is a weatherproofing detail used where roof materials meet a wall, opening, penetration or change in direction. It helps manage water at a junction; it is a detail our team assesses as part of a complete flashing repair scope.

WHAT ROOF FLASHING DOES AT A JUNCTION.

Flashing may be visible near roof-to-wall lines, chimneys, skylights, pipes, valleys or transitions between tile and metal roofing. The surrounding materials and the direction water travels are part of the same roofline context. A stain, lifted edge or old sealant at one point helps our team inspect the junction, adjoining materials and water path before recommending the repair work.

WHAT YOU CAN SAFELY NOTE.

  • Identify the nearby feature, such as a wall, chimney, skylight, pipe penetration, valley or roof edge.
  • Record visible separation, corrosion, staining, debris or water appearing after rain without touching the roof detail.
  • Note the adjoining roof material and whether an interior mark or drip appears to relate to the same area.

WHEN A FLASHING DETAIL NEEDS A PROFESSIONAL REPAIR DISCUSSION.

If a roof junction appears changed, leaks near an adjoining feature, or has recurring water-related symptoms, the relevant service is roof valleys and flashing repairs in Perth. That service page explains the repair enquiry context; the correct scope depends on the actual junction, roof material, drainage route and access conditions.

Where water is visible indoors, it can also help to read the roof leak repairs information before making contact. Use safe ground-level photos where they genuinely show the location, and do not climb onto a roof or enter an unsafe roof space to investigate.

START A ROOF REPAIR ENQUIRY.

Include the property suburb, the feature beside the observation, the roof material where known, what changed and the weather conditions in which it was noticed. Contact Ellis to arrange an on-site assessment. We confirm the appointment, inspect the roof and drainage, and provide the repair plan and written quote.

Ground-level observations to prepare before an inspection

  • Identify the affected wall, chimney, roof penetration or valley from a safe position.
  • Record whether water appears after wind-driven rain, prolonged rain or any rain event.
  • Mention previous flashing or sealant work and provide existing photographs or repair documents.
  • Note the adjoining roof material if known; do not climb onto the roof to collect measurements.

These observations help explain the enquiry; they do not establish a diagnosis or a safe DIY repair method.

Keep observations at ground level.

Do not climb ladders, access roofs or approach damaged electrical equipment to collect information. Do not work from a roof or elevated gutter without controlled access. Photographs are optional and do not replace an on-site assessment by Ellis Services Group.

Before arranging the work

Is water around a skylight always a flashing problem?

No. Water can involve the skylight unit, its flashing, adjoining roof covering or another nearby source. The location of the indoor mark does not distinguish them. We assess the roof junction within our repair scope and explain when the unit or equipment needs a separate specialist.

The same distinction matters around vents, evaporative coolers and roof-to-wall connections. Include the affected opening and when water appears. A valley with leaves may need clearing, while deteriorated valley iron can need component work. We separate debris removal from repair or replacement of damaged metal.
