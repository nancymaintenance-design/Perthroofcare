GUIDE / ROOF REPAIRS

DRAINAGE AFTER RAIN.

After rain, overflow, water marks and ponding can show where roof drainage needs attention. Ellis checks the connected gutter, outlet and downpipe before recommending clearing or repairs.

Start with safe rain-time observations

From ground level, note where water overflows or escapes and whether it continues after rain stops. Keep clear of electrical hazards and do not climb to inspect wet roof edges.

Check the connected drainage route

Leaves may restrict an outlet, but damaged joins, gutter fall and downpipe connections can also affect drainage. Ellis examines the connected route rather than assuming every overflow is a cleaning-only problem.

Arrange a drainage assessment

You can contact Ellis with the location and what you noticed; photos and a complete drainage record are not required. We inspect the roof-edge drainage, explain the findings and confirm the cleaning or repair scope and quote.

PRACTICAL READING / PERTH

Use the detail without jumping to a conclusion.

Rain can make the route from roof surface to gutter, outlet, downpipe and ground connection easier to observe. This guide helps turn that short window into useful notes without asking anyone to work in unsafe conditions. Ellis checks the gutter, outlet and downpipe route on site, identifies the cause and confirms the cleaning or repair plan and quote.

What you can safely observe

  • From a safe covered or ground-level position, identify where water appears to leave the roof edge and enter the gutter line.
  • Watch for water at a gutter corner, outlet, downpipe join, eave, wall or ground connection, and note whether it travels, pools, overflows or lands in an unexpected place.
  • Record the rainfall pattern as best you can: brief shower, steady rain, strong wind with rain or a severe-weather event.
  • After the rain has passed and conditions are safe, compare what you observed with visible water marks, debris, staining or ground drainage paths.

Keep the wider context in view

Drainage is a route rather than a single component. A visible change at a gutter may relate to water arriving from the roof surface, passing an outlet or continuing through a downpipe. Ground levels, leaf litter, tree cover and the placement of connected components can also shape what is noticed. The most useful account distinguishes what was actually seen from what is only suspected.

How to prepare a useful enquiry

A concise record can include the date, rainfall conditions, the side of the property, the gutter or downpipe location, whether water reached the building or ground, and whether the observation has happened before. Photographs or short videos taken from a safe position can help show timing and location. Do not delay an urgent safety response to collect documentation.

When to stop and seek help

Do not climb ladders, access roofs, clear gutters or handle electrical equipment during rain or severe weather. Stay clear of fallen branches, damaged roof materials, overhead electrical infrastructure and water near power. The Bureau of Meteorology publishes current official warnings; use those warnings and local emergency instructions as the priority during severe conditions. When it is safe, use the observation record to make a non-urgent enquiry.
