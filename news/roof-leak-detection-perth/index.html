Roof Leak Detection Perth: Tracing Recurring Leaks After Rain

A recurring roof leak needs its water path traced, not another patch chosen from the ceiling stain alone. Ellis Services Group investigates the roof covering, junctions and drainage above the affected area and repairs the components responsible for water entry. This guide explains how the evidence fits together and why a previous repair can leave a leak unresolved.

Why the ceiling stain can be away from the entry point

Water can move along under-roof layers or building members before reaching a ceiling. A stain identifies the affected room, but the roof directly above it is only part of the investigation. We relate the indoor position to the roof slopes, valleys, walls and penetrations nearby.

Our team checks adjoining tiles or sheets as well as the reported area. This helps distinguish an entry defect from the route water follows after entering. Existing interior photographs support that investigation without requiring the owner to enter the roof space.

Leaks during heavy rain and wind-driven rain

A downpour increases flow through valleys, gutter outlets and downpipes. An obstruction or damaged drainage connection can allow water to back up while the roof covering appears intact. We inspect the connected drainage route alongside laps and flashings.

Wind-driven rain can expose a weak overlap or junction differently from calm rain. Tell us which weather events trigger the leak and whether dripping begins immediately or later. We use that history to focus inspection, then specify work from the physical defects found.

Investigating a leak that returns after repair

A previous patch can leave adjoining corrosion, a deteriorated fixing or a poorly connected flashing untreated. We review the repair area and inspect beyond its edges rather than assuming the same surface needs more sealant.

The repair can involve replacing a damaged tile, compatible fastener work, renewing an affected flashing or restoring drainage. The important distinction is between closing the entry defect and simply covering an indoor symptom. The proposed scope identifies the roof components to be repaired.

From leak investigation to a defined repair scope

Our assessment brings together the affected rooms, rain history, covering material, previous work and accessible roof details. We explain the identified defects, materials and access arrangements before the agreed repairs begin.

Completion checks focus on the repaired connections, adjoining covering and connected drainage. The checks appropriate to the job are agreed with the scope; photographs and records help explain the work completed. Our metal fastener leak-repair case shows the roof condition, fixing details and completed work in a photographed repair sequence.

Related questions

Do I need to wait for another storm before contacting you?

No. Existing water marks, photographs and the timing of previous leaks help us prepare an inspection. Keep clear of wet roof access and report active dripping when you contact our team.

Can you investigate a leak after someone has already repaired it?

We inspect the previous repair and its adjoining roof components, explain the remaining defects and prepare the repair work needed for the current condition.

Ground-level observations to prepare before an inspection

  • Mark the affected room and whether the first sign is staining, dripping or overflow.
  • Record the timing and weather conditions of each event rather than only the latest leak.
  • Keep a record of previous repairs and whether the water-entry pattern changed afterward.
  • Provide safely obtained existing photographs; leave roof access and diagnosis to the inspection.

These observations help explain the enquiry; they do not establish a diagnosis or a safe DIY repair method.

Keep observations at ground level.

Do not climb ladders, access roofs or approach damaged electrical equipment to collect information. Do not work from a roof or elevated gutter without controlled access. Photographs are optional and do not replace an on-site assessment by Ellis Services Group.

Before arranging the work

What if a leak appeared after solar panels were installed?

Tell us when the solar installation and the first leak occurred, and share any earlier repair records. Timing alone does not establish the cause. The roof covering, fixings, penetrations and connected drainage need assessment; electrical equipment must be handled by the appropriate specialist.

A ceiling stain is a starting point, not proof of a particular defect. Note whether water appears in heavy rain, wind-driven rain or dry weather. Water can travel before becoming visible indoors. Where condensation, plumbing or equipment is suspected, the next step may involve another trade rather than a roof patch. Include previous sealant work if the same area is leaking again.
