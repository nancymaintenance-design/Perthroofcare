Service content

Valleys and roof junctions

Valleys carry water where roof slopes meet. Flashings shed it at walls, chimneys, skylights and penetrations. Their formed shape, overlaps and connection to adjoining tiles or sheets matter as much as the visible edge. Clearing deposits, repairing local damage and replacing perforated metal are different scopes.

Our solution

  1. Inspect the channel or junction, uphill water path, adjoining covering and downstream outlet to establish the affected components.
  2. Form the repair or replacement detail for the actual roof material and connection. Agree any tile lifting, access and disposal before work.
  3. Review repaired overlaps, fixings and the connected drainage route. Record limitations where adjoining material or equipment needs separate work.

A skylight leak may involve roof flashing, the skylight itself or glazing. Roof repairs do not automatically include skylight replacement, vents, whirlybirds or air-conditioning equipment work.

Scope details

Roof flashing at walls, openings and junctions

Flashing directs water away from junctions around walls, chimneys, skylights and roof penetrations. Ellis checks its condition and connections to the adjoining roof, then confirms the junction repair and quote.

Valley clearing, local repair and replacement criteria

Cleaning suits debris in an otherwise sound valley. Corrosion, damaged lining or faulty connections can require repair or replacement. Ellis checks the valley and surrounding roof surfaces before quoting for the work.

Skylight flashing and uphill water paths

A leak near a skylight can involve its flashing, the adjoining tiles or sheets, or water arriving from higher on the roof. Ellis inspects the connected roof area and traces the entry path before confirming the repair.
