Project story updated 2026-10-09

A ridge junction within the metal roof covering

This corrugated roof has a formed metal connection where the roof lines meet. The project photographs show the ridge run in its wider setting and the junction from closer viewpoints. The surrounding sheets remain visible throughout the sequence, making it possible to see how the completed flashing belongs to the roof covering rather than standing apart from it.

Our ridge flashing and connection work

The completed work shows grey formed metal along the ridge and across the adjoining junction. Close photographs show the connection, cap edges and screw-fixed line beside the corrugated sheets. Another view follows the longer ridge run. This sequence presents both the detailed workmanship at the intersection and the finished roof line extending beyond that point.

The junction, overlap and fixing line together

A ridge connection brings formed metal, adjoining sheets and fixing positions into the same detail. Its shape changes with the roof geometry, so the intersection must connect with the continuing cap run. The photographs explain that relationship through several angles. They also show why metal roof repairs should address the connected components rather than treating a single fixing as the whole job.

The finished ridge detail and our service scope

The completed views show the grey ridge flashing following the roof line, with its junction and nearby sheet profile clearly visible. They provide a practical example of the formed metal work we undertake. Our metal roof and flashing repair services cover ridge details, cap overlaps, affected fixing points and adjoining connections, with the repair scope organised for the property.
