OUR PROJECT / ELLIS SERVICES GROUP

Project story updated 2026-10-09

A corrugated roof with connected ridge and hip lines

This roof brings several corrugated planes together beneath formed grey metal capping. The project photographs show the overall roof, a close installation view, the finished cap run and an eave connection. Together they explain the job more clearly than an isolated close-up: the repaired roof junction belongs to a continuous covering, with adjoining sheets and drainage at its edges.

Our capping installation and fixing work

The work-stage photograph shows our tradesperson securing the formed metal capping over the corrugated roof junction. The completed views show the cap continuing along the roof line, with its fixing points visible beside the sheet profile. Our work on these connections joins the cap to the surrounding covering and gives the junction a defined, consistent finish.

Why the cap-to-sheet connection matters

Ridge and hip capping cover the meeting point between roof planes. The formed metal, adjoining corrugations and fixing positions work together at that junction. A repair needs attention to the whole connection, including the direction of the cap run and the surrounding sheets. The close photographs make these workmanship details visible alongside the wider completed roof.

Completed roof lines and the eave detail

The finished project shows grey capping following the ridge and hip lines, with a separate close view of the roof edge beside the gutter. These images present both the central roof junction and its surrounding roofline. For owners needing similar work, our metal roof repairs service covers capping, affected sheets, fixings and flashing connections within a coordinated repair scope.
