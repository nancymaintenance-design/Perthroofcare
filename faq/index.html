Leaks, stains and previous repairs

Why does my roof only leak during heavy rain?

Heavy rain can reveal a water-entry or drainage problem not apparent in lighter rain. Note the timing and affected room so the connected roof and drainage can be assessed.

Read the related service explanation

Why does my roof leak when it is windy?

Wind can change how rain reaches roof connections. An inspection needs to relate the weather pattern to the covering, laps and flashings rather than assuming one cause.

Read the related service explanation

What causes ceiling stains after rain?

A rain-related stain may indicate water entry, but the ceiling mark alone cannot identify the source. Record when it appears and arrange an assessment of the relevant roof area.

Read the related service explanation

Does a ceiling stain always mean the roof is leaking?

No. Roof water entry, plumbing, equipment or condensation can be relevant. Timing and location help direct the assessment; a stain is not a diagnosis.

Read the related service explanation

Can water travel before appearing on the ceiling?

Yes. Water may travel along connected materials before becoming visible. The inspection should consider the water path, not only the spot directly above the stain.

Read the related service explanation

Why is the visible stain away from the damaged roof area?

The entry point and visible stain can differ because water follows connected surfaces. The route needs assessment before a particular roof defect is blamed.

Read the related service explanation

Why is my roof still leaking after a repair?

A recurring leak can involve an unresolved source, a connected defect or changed conditions. Provide the previous work records and new leak timing before another repair is specified.

Read the related service explanation

Why has roof sealant not stopped the leak?

Sealant cannot substitute for identifying the source and assessing adjoining components. A recurring leak needs investigation before another surface patch.

Read the related service explanation

Tiles and ridge capping

Can a cracked roof tile cause a leak?

A cracked tile can allow water entry, but its position and connected details matter. Inspect the affected tiles and adjoining roof before setting the repair scope.

Read the related service explanation

Can individual roof tiles be replaced?

Yes, where the surrounding roof and matching material support a local repair. The tile profile, fit and availability need checking before replacement is confirmed.

Read the related service explanation

What is the difference between roof repointing and rebedding?

Repointing addresses exposed pointing, while rebedding renews the supporting bed. The stability and condition of the ridge determine which work is needed.

Read the related service explanation

How do I know if ridge capping needs repair?

Visible cracks, missing mortar or displaced ridge tiles warrant assessment. The condition of the support and surrounding tiles determines the repair, not the crack alone.

Read the related service explanation

Valleys, flashings and metal roofs

What is a roof valley?

A valley directs water where roof slopes meet. Its channel, adjoining tiles and downstream drainage need to be considered together.

Read the related service explanation

When does valley iron need replacing?

Replacement may be appropriate where deterioration means the valley material cannot support a suitable repair. Cleaning alone does not repair damaged metal.

Read the related service explanation

What does roof flashing do?

Flashing manages water where roof materials meet another surface or opening. The formed detail and adjoining connections work together.

Read the related service explanation

What are the signs of damaged roof flashing?

Lifted or separated edges, corrosion and rain-related stains can indicate a flashing problem. The junction and surrounding roof need inspection to establish the cause.

Read the related service explanation

Can roof screws and washers cause leaks?

Yes. Fixings, washers and the surrounding metal can contribute to water entry. Assess the whole fixing point before deciding whether replacement alone is suitable.

Read the related service explanation

Can a metal roof be repaired without replacing every sheet?

Often a local scope can be considered, depending on the sheet and connected components. Widespread deterioration may call for a broader assessment.

Read the related service explanation

Is a leaking skylight caused by the skylight or the roof around it?

Either may be relevant. The roof junction and the skylight need to be distinguished before the work is agreed; a roof repair is not automatically skylight replacement.

Read the related service explanation

Drainage and other moisture sources

Can overflowing gutters cause water to enter the roof space?

Overflow can affect adjoining building details. The gutter, outlet and downpipe need a connected assessment; clearing alone may not resolve a recurring problem.

Read the related service explanation

What causes a box gutter to overflow?

Blockages, outlet restrictions, deterioration or drainage limitations can be relevant. Assess the box gutter and its connected outlets and overflow route before choosing the repair.

Read the related service explanation

Is it a roof leak, condensation or a plumbing leak?

Timing helps direct investigation but is not conclusive. Roof water entry, condensation and plumbing or equipment issues need distinguishing before a repair is prescribed.

Read the related service explanation

Repair, restoration and replacement

What is the difference between roof repair and roof restoration?

Repair targets a defect. Restoration combines suitable repairs with agreed preparation or finishing; it is not automatically necessary for a local leak.

Read the related service explanation

Does roof painting fix roof leaks?

Painting alone does not establish that a roof leak has been repaired. Identify and correct the relevant defects before the agreed cleaning, preparation and spray-painting stages.

Read the related service explanation

When is roof replacement more suitable than repair?

Replacement may be more suitable when the roof cannot support an appropriate repair. Ask for the findings and a clear comparison of retained and replaced components.

Read the related service explanation

Is a second roof assessment worthwhile?

It can help clarify an unexplained recurring leak or different repair proposals. Agree the assessment scope and fee, and provide earlier records.

Read the related service explanation

Inspection, quotes and winter preparation

What should a roof inspection include?

Confirm the purpose, accessible components, limitations, inspection fee and available records. A roof assessment is not automatically a whole-building, engineering or insurance report.

Read the related service explanation

What should I look for in a roof repair quote?

Check the repair items, materials, access, preparation, waste removal, exclusions and approval of additional work. Confirm inspection charges and applicable written terms separately.

Read the related service explanation

How should I prepare my roof for Perth’s winter rain?

Record previous leaks and arrange relevant roof and drainage checks before winter. Stay at ground level; the property condition determines maintenance priorities.

Read the related service explanation

What information should I send when reporting a roof leak?

Include the location, timing, affected area, known roof material and previous repairs. Existing safe photographs are optional; identify the person authorised to arrange access.

Roof leak or condensation: what should I record?

Rain-related water entry and moisture occurring in dry weather are different observations, but neither pattern is a diagnosis on its own. Water can travel before it becomes visible. Condensation, plumbing and equipment can also be relevant, so a ceiling stain alone does not establish that the roof needs sealing.

Note the timing and location safely

  • Does the dampness follow rain, appear only in heavy rain, or occur during dry weather too?
  • Is it in one room or several areas, and is nearby plumbing or equipment in use?
  • Is the mark new, recurring or changing after a previous repair?

Existing photographs and a short timing record help explain the enquiry. Keep observations at ground level and away from damaged electrical equipment. Do not enter a roof space or climb onto the roof to identify the source.

Does moisture on a dry day rule out a roof leak?

No. The timing of a visible mark alone cannot confirm or exclude the source. Explain when it appears, recent rain, any previous repairs and nearby plumbing or equipment so the enquiry can be directed to the appropriate assessment.

Should I book painting to cover a ceiling stain or stop dampness?

A surface finish does not identify or correct the moisture source. First arrange the appropriate investigation and address the cause. Our roof leak repair service and water-entry investigation guide explain the roof-related assessment.

Does dampness without rain always need a roof repair?

No. Condensation, plumbing or equipment can also be relevant. Note whether moisture follows rain or occurs in dry weather and explain any nearby equipment. Is water around a skylight always a flashing problem?

No. Water can involve the skylight unit, its flashing, adjoining roof covering or another nearby source. The location of the indoor mark does not distinguish them.
